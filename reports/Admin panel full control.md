# Make the admin panel the store's truth

For Nansei Organics, "full admin control" means one thing: every price, size, stock count, legal detail and piece of shop-front copy that a shopper sees, and that the server charges for, comes from data the owner can edit in the admin panel. Nothing should come from arrays copied by hand into HTML files. Today the panel does not reach that standard. It reliably edits the basic fields of database products, moves orders forward, moderates reviews, publishes blog and news posts, and (new in the working tree) edits store settings. But shoppers mostly see four hard-coded product catalogues that disagree with each other. Stock is never enforced. The server accepts browser-supplied prices for the 16 real products. And the admin page itself renders customer-typed order fields as raw HTML, next to an admin token kept in `localStorage`. The work already started is the right foundation. It covers a store-settings document (delivery fee, free-delivery threshold, COD and COD fee, announcement bar, contact, GSTIN, FSSAI number and grievance officer), its admin Settings page, account lockout after 5 wrong passwords, 12-hour admin sessions, and `backend/data/catalog.json`, which holds 16 products with explicit per-size price and MRP. The rest should follow the order of harm. First, close the admin-panel XSS and the broken screens. Next, move the catalogue into MongoDB and make the server the only authority on price and stock. Then switch each storefront page to a shared data loader that survives Render cold starts. Only after that comes the larger editing surface: variants, images, GST invoices, coupons, homepage content, an audit log and roles. Indian law turns several of these from nice-to-have into obligations: GST Rule 46 invoices, the FSSAI number on bills, the seller-identity and grievance-officer duties of the E-Commerce Rules, and Legal Metrology declarations on every product page. About fifteen inputs can only come from the owner. The largest are confirming the extracted catalogue and stock, GST registration and per-product HSN rates, the manufacturer and packer details, and whether the hero's marketing claims can be documented.

**Scope and method.** This report synthesises three research notes written on 12 Nov 2026: a code audit of the working tree, a review of admin best practice and Indian compliance, and a design for a data-driven storefront. It also builds on the earlier checkout, reviews and journal report. I spot-checked the working tree again while writing. That confirmed the lockout and 12-hour session code, the Settings form and `catalog.json`. It also confirmed that the Discounts functions are still undefined and that customer order fields are still unescaped. Other sessions were editing the same files, so line numbers are approximate: search for the function name before editing. Neither the backend nor the production database was run or queried. Legal points rest mostly on secondary summaries of the rules, as flagged inline, and none of this is legal or tax advice.

## Full control means one source of truth, not more buttons

Large platforms show how wide "full control" can be. Shopify splits store permissions into about twenty areas, including Orders, Products, Inventory, Customers, Discounts, Content, Files, Online store, Store settings and Finance. Within each area it separates View, Create and edit, Delete and Export, and it marks tax documents and data requests as sensitive ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/permissions/store-permissions)). Shopify also grants permissions to the whole store, never to individual records. That points to a modest design for a one-to-three-person store: about ten modules and two or three coarse roles. The modules are Products with weight variants, Inventory, Orders, Customers, Discounts, Content, Settings, Reviews, Reports and Staff. WooCommerce gives a minimal but complete model for inventory. It manages stock per item, holds stock on pending payments, sets separate low-stock and out-of-stock thresholds with email alerts, and lets the owner hide sold-out items and choose whether to show counts ([WooCommerce docs](https://woocommerce.com/document/configuring-woocommerce-settings/products/)). Zoho Commerce's India edition shows the tax side: the owner enters the GSTIN and legal name once, each item carries its own HSN code, and rates apply through tax rules ([Zoho Commerce Help](https://www.zoho.com/commerce/help/Taxes/); [Zoho Invoice Help](https://www.zoho.com/in/invoice/help/gst/)).

For Nansei, the data falls into three kinds, plus the operations and guardrails around them. The **catalogue** is products with sizes, per-size stock, compliance fields and SEO, along with categories and coupons. The **store-settings singleton** is already built. It holds contact, shipping, COD, announcement, social and legal details, and still needs a GST rate, a delivery estimate and invoice settings. The **content blocks and pages** are the hero slides, homepage sections, policy pages, video reels and per-page SEO. Around these sit order operations (status, COD confirmation, cancel, refund, invoices) and guardrails (escaping, audit log, soft delete, roles). The test of "full control" is end to end. An edit in the admin must change what every page shows *and* what the server charges. Today that test fails for almost everything except settings, because the backend and four browser-side catalogues are separate sources of truth ([index.html](pages/index.html); [product.html](pages/product.html); [productController.js](backend/controllers/productController.js)).

## The admin edits a few things reliably, and shoppers see fewer of them

The audit sorted the nine admin sections into three groups. Five work end to end. **Products** works for basic fields only. **Orders** works for forward status changes only. **Customers** is read-only. **Reviews** handles reply and hide-with-reason, and **Settings** is the newly built section. Two are half-broken. In **Blogs** and **News**, Edit and Preview look posts up in `localStorage`, so editing a post from the API silently publishes a duplicate, and the date column prints "undefined". The **Dashboard** mixes real numbers with invented ones. Its "↑ 12.5% vs last month" labels are static HTML, still present in the working copy. Its revenue chart plots fixed figures for Aug–Dec. It covers only the latest 50 orders, because `GET /orders` defaults to `limit = 50`. And it never counts COD revenue, because marking an order Delivered does not set `isPaid` ([orderRoutes.js](backend/routes/orderRoutes.js)). The working `GET /orders/admin/stats` endpoint with monthly revenue is never called. **Discounts** throws a ReferenceError on open: `renderDiscounts()` and `openDiscountModal()` are called but still not defined anywhere in [admin-panel.html](pages/admin-panel.html). The Ship modal fills in a random tracking number from `generateTracking()`, so the admin can mark an order shipped with an invented AWB. The Shiprocket endpoint `POST /api/shipping/:id/ship` exists but no UI calls it ([shippingRoutes.js](backend/routes/shippingRoutes.js)).

The backend can do more than the UI exposes. It has endpoints, but no screens, for order stats, Shiprocket booking, user role changes and deletes, discount CRUD, category and combo creation. Some write paths are broken or missing at the API level. Coupon creation fails every time, because the route sends `maxUses` and `expiresAt` while the schema requires `validFrom`, `validUntil` and `usageLimit`. `usedCount` is never incremented, so usage limits are never enforced ([couponRoutes.js](backend/routes/couponRoutes.js); [Coupon.js](backend/models/Coupon.js)). The product controller's whitelist drops `quantityOptions`, `benefits`, `howToUse`, `metaTitle`, `metaDescription` and `certifications`. It accepts `sku`, but the schema has no `sku` field, so the SKU column always shows "—". An old price, once set, cannot be cleared ([productController.js](backend/controllers/productController.js)). The Discount model is read nowhere by the storefront or the order guard, so it is dead code.

What shoppers actually see after an admin edit is the bigger problem:

| Admin action | Homepage (API up) | `product.html?id=<Mongo id>` | `product.html?id=1..16` | Wishlist / account | Server at checkout |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change price | New price within ~30 s | New price, but sold "per piece" | Old static price | Old price, or item vanishes | Accepts the cart's old price if ≥20% of the database price; any price for ids 1–16 |
| Stock to 0 | Still buyable | No out-of-stock state | — | — | Order accepted; stock never decremented |
| Mark inactive | Hidden | Still viewable and buyable | — | — | Order accepted |
| Sizes, benefits, SEO | Not possible: no UI, and the controller drops them | — | Static | — | — |

The root cause is the catalogue. It is hard-coded in `index.html`, `product.html`, `wishlist.html` and `account.html`, plus a dead `js/products-data.js`, and the copies disagree on names, images and even what id 11 is ([index.html](pages/index.html); [product.html](pages/product.html); [wishlist.html](pages/wishlist.html)). Every page prices sizes in the browser from multipliers. Rice is priced per kg, but "Other Products" use a 250 g base. An admin-created rice product falls to the default config and is sold per piece. Most damaging, the order guard checks prices only for valid MongoDB ids, and only against a 20% floor. Carts from the real storefront carry numeric ids 1–16, so **the server accepts whatever price the browser sends** and re-checks only the arithmetic ([orderGuard.js](backend/utils/orderGuard.js); [Order.js](backend/models/Order.js)). The homepage also invents a strike-through "old price" at `price × 1.25` for API products without one ([index.html](pages/index.html)). Beyond products, these are static too: the hero slider and its claims ("25 Acres", "22 years", "9 internationally recognised certifications", "200+ farming families"), the homepage sections, the "Only 32 Left" scarcity line, a countdown that always runs to midnight, the policy pages, the blog's 16 inline posts, the video reels (third-party sample clips), categories (a fixed schema enum), the "GST (5%)" label, the "today + 5 days" delivery estimate, and every page's SEO. The contact phone and email on the checkout, news and policy pages also sit outside the new settings hooks.

**Already done in the working tree.** The `Setting` singleton (`key: 'store'`) stores `storeName`; contact phone, WhatsApp, email and address; legal business name, GSTIN and FSSAI; grievance officer name, email and phone; shipping `freeShippingAbove`, `shippingFee`, `codEnabled`, `codFee` and `dispatchNote`; announcement items with an optional promo code and link; social links; and `updatedBy` ([Setting.js](backend/models/Setting.js)). The public `GET /api/settings`, admin `GET /api/settings/admin` and `PUT /api/settings` routes validate and upsert the document, and clear a 60-second in-memory cache ([settingsRoutes.js](backend/routes/settingsRoutes.js); [settings.js](backend/utils/settings.js)). Checkout pricing and the COD on/off switch and fee now read these rules through `GET /api/orders/pricing` and `orderGuard`. The admin panel has a Settings form, including the grievance-officer fields and a help line explaining the legal duty. `js/site-shell.js` applies cached settings from `localStorage` at once, then refreshes them, filling in the footer contact, FSSAI line and social links, and `index.html` swaps the announcement bar for the admin's items. On login, after five wrong passwords an account waits 15 minutes. The counter sits on the user document, so it works across IP addresses, and a correct login resets it ([authController.js](backend/controllers/authController.js)). Admin JWTs now expire after 12 hours (`ADMIN_JWT_EXPIRE`), and customers keep 7 days ([User.js](backend/models/User.js)). `backend/data/catalog.json` holds all 16 products by `legacyId`. Each product has a name, category, tagline, description, images, how-to-use, shelf life, country of origin, and sizes with stable SKUs (`P1-500G`), explicit `price` and `mrp`, and `stock: null`. Id 11 is recorded as "Butterfly Pea Flower (Sangu Poo)". That suggests the homepage's "Sangupoo" is the same product under its Tamil name, not a second product. Tests exist for auth, orders, reviews, settings and shipping. None of this is committed yet. The catalogue file is not yet read by any code, and every stock value is unknown.

## Indian law turns a dozen fields into obligations

Four rule sets decide what the admin must hold and what the site must show. Under **GST Rule 46**, a tax invoice must carry the following ([GSTZen, secondary copy of the rule](https://gstzen.in/a/tax-invoice-cgst-rule-46.html)):

- the supplier's name, address and GSTIN;
- a serial number of up to 16 characters, unique within the financial year;
- the date;
- the recipient's details, which are required for unregistered buyers only at ₹50,000 or more, or when the buyer asks;
- for each line, the HSN code, description, quantity with unit, taxable value after discount, rate and tax amount;
- the place of supply with the state name for inter-state supplies;
- whether reverse charge applies;
- a signature, which is waived for electronic invoices.

Businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore need 4-digit HSN codes, which are mandatory on B2B invoices ([GimBooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/gst-invoice-mandatory-fields-rule-46-checklist/); [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-invoice-format-mandatory-fields-rules-and-examples-for-businesses/)). Since 1 January 2022, an **FSSAI order** has required the 14-digit licence or registration number on every receipt, invoice, cash memo and bill ([FSSAI order](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2021/10/6156936ac80bbOrder_License_Number_Print_01_10_2021.pdf); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/fssai-food-businesses-mention-license-no-invoices-w-e-f-1st-october-2021.html)). A December 2024 advisory says e-commerce food businesses must display valid licence numbers ([FSSAI advisory](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2024/12/674efa161d756Adobe%20Scan%203%20Dec%202024.pdf)). That advisory is a scanned PDF and its exact wording was not read. The **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020** require the seller to show its legal name, principal address, website, customer-care email and phone, and a grievance officer. The officer must acknowledge complaints within 48 hours and resolve them within a month. An inventory seller such as a D2C store must also show return, refund, exchange, delivery, payment, payment-security and cancellation terms, and a total price that breaks out delivery and taxes. The rules also bar it from posing as a consumer to post reviews ([IndiaLaw summary](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)). **Legal Metrology rule 6(10)**, in force online since 1 January 2018, requires these declarations on the product page ([Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/aiming-greater-transparency-on-sale-of-packaged-commodity-online)):

- the manufacturer's, packer's or importer's name and address;
- the country of origin for imports;
- the common or generic name;
- the net quantity in standard units;
- the best-before date where applicable;
- the MRP, inclusive of all taxes.

A 2026 amendment inserting a further sub-rule took effect on 1 July 2026, and its text was not read ([FoxMandal copy of the Gazette](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legal-Metrology-Packaged-Commodities-Amendment-Rules-2026.pdf)).

The new Settings document already covers the seller-identity half of this: legal name, address, phone, email, GSTIN, FSSAI number and grievance officer. Three gaps remain. First, the **product-level fields are missing**. `catalog.json` has `shelfLife` and `countryOfOrigin`, but no HSN code, GST rate, net quantity per pack, or manufacturer and packer name and address. The admin should refuse to publish a product while any of these is empty, and the product page should render them in a "Product information" block. Second, **GST is a flat 5% constant** in both `orderGuard.js` and `pricingFrom()`, and the catalogue is mixed. A CBIC ready reckoner of September 2025 lists pre-packaged, labelled rice (HSN 1006) and wheat flour (HSN 1101) at 5% total, and the loose, unbranded versions at Nil ([CBIC Ready Reckoner via a2ztaxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/CBIC-GST-Ready-Reckoner-indicating-updated-Central-Goods-and-Services-Tax-CGST-rates-on-goods.pdf); [West Bengal rate chart, Apr 2026](https://comtax.wb.gov.in/GST/GST_PPU/CONSOLIDATED%20GST%20RATE%20CHART%20OF%20GOODS_20260416.pdf)). Secondary sites list HSN 0603 cut flowers, including dried ones, at Nil ([RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/hsn/gst-rate-hsn-code-0603)). But 0603 covers flowers for bouquets or ornament. Edible dried flowers sold for tea, such as butterfly pea, hibiscus and avarampoo, may sit in another chapter at another rate. Rice flour is HSN 1102 ([Aditya Birla Capital](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/chapter-11-milling-products-malt-starches-inulin-wheat-gluten-gst-rate-hsn-code)), and the rates for the rose milk, sherbet, health mix and fig powder were not researched. So **GST must be stored per product, with a CA confirming each code and rate**, and invoices must carry per-line rates. Third, **there is no invoice at all**. The order modal shows a "Subtotal" equal to the total, with no GST, delivery, COD fee or coupon. The invoice generator needs these pieces. It should assign a sequential number per financial year (for example `NSO/2026-27/00042`) at confirmation or dispatch, never reuse one, and cancel through a credit note rather than deletion. It should split tax as CGST plus SGST when the buyer's state matches the seller's and IGST otherwise, taking the place of supply from the shipping state. And it should print the FSSAI number and "Reverse charge: No". Settings therefore needs a "GST registered" switch, so that an unregistered store issues a bill of supply instead, plus the seller's state and code and an invoice prefix. The registration threshold itself was not researched.

Two legal points reach beyond the invoice. The first is that **the MRP must include all taxes** under Legal Metrology, while today's checkout adds 5% GST on top of the shown price. The earlier report left "GST-inclusive shelf prices or GST added at checkout" to the owner. This rule tips that decision towards inclusive prices, or at least an inclusive MRP. A lawyer or CA should confirm how the two fit together. The second is that **honesty is now a compliance property of the admin itself**. The no-fake-reviews duty means review tools should offer approve, hide-with-reason and reply, and never "write as customer" or edit a customer's words. The working tree already removed admin edits of reviews. The static "Only 32 Left" line and the midnight countdown are the kind of false urgency the CCPA's 2023 dark-patterns guidelines target ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/consumer-trading-unfair-trading/1398262/ccpa-issues-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023-effective-30-november-2023)). Scarcity shown on the site should therefore come from real stock, or not appear. The same applies to the hero's certification and family counts: once they become admin-editable content, the owner is publishing them as fact.

## Make the server authoritative before making pages dynamic

The safe path to a data-driven storefront runs in a strict order. **The first step changes nothing visible.** The server prices every order line from the database, so stale or tampered browser prices are refused. That gives the owner's edits real effect before any page is rewritten. Each product gets an embedded `variants[]` array, with `sku`, `label`, `size {value, unit}`, `price`, `mrp`, `stock` and `isActive`. It also gets a unique `legacyId` (1–16), a `slug`, the compliance fields and a `content` block for the rich product-page sections. The top-level `price` and `oldPrice` survive only as values derived in a `pre('save')` hook, so existing sorting keeps working. This follows Shopify's model, where price, compare-at price, SKU and inventory belong to the variant ([Shopify ProductVariant](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/ProductVariant)). It also follows MongoDB's guidance that fields updated together belong in one document, so the stock update is atomic ([MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/tutorial/model-data-for-atomic-operations/)). `catalog.json` is the migration source. A re-runnable script, run after a `mongodump` backup, upserts each product by `legacyId`. Because the file's sizes reproduce today's formula prices, no shopper sees a price change on switch-over day. `GET /api/products/:id` then accepts an ObjectId, a legacy number or a slug, so old `?id=1` links work for good, and it returns 404 for inactive products unless the caller is an admin.

The order guard then changes in four ways. It resolves legacy ids and ObjectIds in one query. It finds each line's variant by `sku`, or by `legacyId` plus the size label for carts saved before the switch. It sets the price from the database and rejects inactive or unknown items. And it decrements stock with the documented pattern of putting the stock condition in the filter: `updateOne({_id, variants: {$elemMatch: {sku, stock: {$gte: qty}}}}, {$inc: {'variants.$.stock': -qty}})`. A `modifiedCount` of 0 means the item is out of stock. Stock comes back on cancellation, and coupon `usedCount` increments the same way. Multi-line orders need either compensating increments on failure or a transaction. The exact positional form should be checked against the MongoDB manual. The existing ₹1.5 total check and its 409 "prices changed" response stay. The pure-function design of `orderGuard.js` makes all of this testable without a database, extending `orders.test.js`. One hazard comes with real stock: every value in `catalog.json` is `null`, so the owner must count inventory before enforcement is switched on.

**Pages switch next, and they must never wait on Render.** A free Render service sleeps after 15 idle minutes and takes about a minute to wake ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). Netlify's proxy gives up after 26 seconds ([Netlify](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/routing/redirects/rewrites-proxies/)). So a page that blocks on the API after a quiet spell fails outright. A shared `js/catalog.js` should render from the first layer it has. First is a deploy-time snapshot (`data/storefront.json`, plus `data/products/<slug>.json` for rich content). Next is the last API response saved in `localStorage`. Last is a background fetch of a single `GET /api/storefront`, which returns settings, the light catalogue and a version number, with a 10-second abort. The snapshot belongs under `/data/`, not `/js/`. The repo's `netlify.toml` marks `/js/*.js` immutable for a year, while Netlify's default for other static files revalidates on every deploy ([Netlify caching](https://docs.netlify.com/build/caching/caching-overview/)). `site-shell.js` should ping `/api/health` early so Render starts waking while the visitor browses. Pages switch in rising order of risk: wishlist, account, home, the 7,700-line product page, and finally checkout, which starts sending `{product, sku, quantity}`. Each switch is one revertable commit, smoke-tested with `tests/ui/pagecheck.js`, and each deletes that page's inline array. Two refinements are cheap. The first is a `Netlify-CDN-Cache-Control: public, s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=86400` header on public GETs, which Netlify supports ([Netlify caching](https://docs.netlify.com/build/caching/caching-overview/)); whether it applies to *proxied* responses must be checked with `curl -I`. The second is keeping the last good settings in memory rather than falling back to the hard-coded defaults during a database blip. Public cache headers must never reach `/api/cart`, `/api/orders/*` (except `/pricing`), `/api/auth/*`, `/api/users/*` or `/api/settings/admin`.

**Images and content come last.** No upload code exists. The admin pastes URLs, and assets are spread across two ImageKit accounts and Unsplash. The secure pattern is an admin-only `GET /api/uploads/auth` that returns a single-use `token`, an `expire` time and an HMAC-SHA1 `signature`, computed on the server with the private key ([ImageKit Node SDK](https://github.com/imagekit-developer/imagekit-nodejs)). The browser then uploads straight to ImageKit, and the backend saves `{url, fileId, alt}` only if the URL starts with the configured endpoint and folder. ImageKit's signature covers only the token and expiry, so a 5-minute lifetime and that URL check carry the security. Hero slides fit the Settings document as a capped `heroSlides[]` array, with `ctaHref` checked to be relative or `https://`. Homepage sections, policy pages, reels and per-page SEO need a generic `Content` model: a key, JSON data, sanitised HTML, SEO fields, `isPublished` and `updatedBy`. Draft and preview should come with it.

## Lock the admin down before widening it

The lockout and the 12-hour session are real gains. OWASP's advice is to throttle failed logins per account rather than per IP, and to keep the reset flow working for a locked account ([OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html)). The new code does both. Two refinements remain. OWASP prefers growing delays to a fixed lockout, and it wants the same generic response whether the password is wrong or the account is locked. The current 429 "Too many wrong passwords" message tells an attacker that the address belongs to a real account. OWASP also calls MFA "by far the best defense" against password attacks ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html)). TOTP with recovery codes therefore belongs on the roadmap. It moves earlier the moment a second person gets admin access.

The urgent gap is elsewhere. Order line names come from the customer's request body, and address fields are only trimmed. The admin panel still interpolates `${p.name}` and `${o.address}` into `innerHTML` in the order list and the order modal. Only the Reviews section uses `escAdmin`. A crafted order name such as `<img src=x onerror=…>` would run in the owner's session and could read the admin JWT from `localStorage` ([orderGuard.js](backend/utils/orderGuard.js); [admin-panel.html](pages/admin-panel.html)). The 12-hour expiry shortens that token's value to an attacker, but does not remove it. **Escaping every interpolated field in the admin panel is the first item of work.** The second is moving the token into an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie, with a token version on the user so that a password change or logout revokes sessions. Today logout does nothing on the server. Server-side role checks are already consistent. Every admin route uses `protect` plus `authorize('admin')`, and the role is re-read from the database on each request. But the stricter `adminLimiter` covers only `/api/users`, and every limiter is skipped when `NODE_ENV=development` ([server.js](backend/server.js)). That makes a correct production `NODE_ENV` a live security setting.

Beyond that, add an append-only `AuditLog` collection, written by a helper on every admin write and login: `{actor, action, entity, entityId, before, after, ip, at}`. Shopify merchants rely on third-party apps for exactly this, because the built-in log is shallow ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/staff-activity-logs-what-are-you-using/133900)). Products, coupons, banners and posts should get soft delete with a Trash view, replacing today's hard product delete. Orders and invoices should never be deleted, only cancelled, because GST records must be kept. Destructive actions should get a confirm dialog that names the item. The server should check that selling price ≤ MRP and stock ≥ 0, and warn on any price change over 50%. Roles should stay coarse: Owner, Operations (orders, stock, customers) and Content (products, content, reviews). Any admin can currently promote any user through an unaudited API call. That should become Owner-only, and should require the current password again.

| 7 | P2 | Fix coupons (field names, PUT and DELETE, `showOnSite`) and link the announcement codes to real coupons. Keep or delete the Discount model. Make categories editable | Yes |
| 8 | P2 | `Content` model for hero slides, homepage sections, policies and reels, with draft and preview. Contact `data-` hooks on the checkout, news and policy pages | Yes (owner sign-off) |
| 9 | P2 | Audit log and viewer, soft delete and Trash, Owner/Ops/Content roles, HttpOnly cookie with revocation, TOTP for admins | No |
| 10 | P3 | Reports: sales by period, COD versus prepaid, RTO rate, GST CSV for the accountant. CDN cache headers. Choose a paid Render instance or a pinger. Consolidate ImageKit accounts | No |

COD confirmation sits in phase 6, not later, because it is the main lever Indian D2C tooling uses against return-to-origin losses. The supporting figures are vendor claims: Shiprocket reports a 45% cut in RTO losses from WhatsApp workflows ([Tata Communications/Shiprocket](https://tatacommunications.com/hubfs/47271964/TaCO-2024/case-studies/doc/Shiprocket-WhatsApp.pdf)), and an aggregator, without a source, reports 34–41% for medium-risk orders ([Shipmozo](https://www.shipmozo.com/blog/cod-verification-whatsapp-reduce-cod-fraud)). A manual "Confirmed by call or WhatsApp" step recorded on the order captures most of that value with no integration. No independent data ranks admin features by return on investment for stores this size, so the order above is a judgement built on legal duties, revenue risk and security.

| Invoices | Issue at confirmation or at dispatch; invoice prefix | Fixes when the FY serial is consumed |

## Conclusion

The working tree has reached a turning point. Settings showed that a single admin-edited document can drive the server's prices and the site's footer at the same time. `catalog.json` shows the catalogue can be one file instead of four disagreeing arrays. The remaining risk is the order of work. If the variant editor, image upload and homepage blocks are built before the server prices from the database, the owner gains buttons whose effects shoppers never see and the checkout never charges. That would repeat the "Changes are live on the site" toast that is only partly true today. Built in the order above, each step makes an existing admin action real before it adds a new one.

The less obvious implication is that Indian compliance and data-driven design are the same project. HSN codes, tax-inclusive MRPs, manufacturer details, FSSAI numbers on invoices and a named grievance officer are all fields. They belong in Settings and on each variant, and they come out automatically on product pages and invoices. A store whose truth lives in one database can satisfy those rules with validation. A store whose truth lives in copied HTML can satisfy them only by vigilance. The same holds for honesty: once scarcity, ratings and claims come only from real data, the admin cannot overstate them by accident.
