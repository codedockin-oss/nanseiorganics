# Replace tabs and health claims with checkable facts

The Nansei product page should be one readable column of facts, not a box of tabs full of benefits. On a phone the order is gallery, name and one-line descriptor, pack-size buttons, a price block that shows the per-kg price, Add to Cart, then a delivery and returns line. Below that come three or four highlights and stacked sections (About, How to cook, Storage and shelf life), with only About open, then the Product facts and label declarations, the reviews and one related row. On desktop the buy box sits beside the gallery and the same sections sit fully expanded underneath. Each description should be built from facts a shopper could check: variety, season, grain colour and size, raw or parboiled, soak time and water ratio, the dishes it is used for, and the pack's legal declarations. The old page failed on every count. About 62% of its phone height was filler, the description was 12 px text behind five truncated tabs, 9 of the 16 descriptions were written for search engines ("many people search for…"), and the benefit lists made disease claims ("Controls Blood Sugar", "Diabetes Control") that FSSAI's 2018 claims regulations prohibit outright. A shipped fallback even carried a fabricated diabetic testimonial. Most of the structure is now fixed. The tabs have become sections plus a Product facts table, and the story circles, invented trust blocks, fake blog strip and two filler related rows are gone. Rose Milk and Idiyappam Flour now show a "photo coming soon" card in place of stock photos. The biggest job left is the words themselves. The per-product copy file that the new sections read from (`js/product-copy.js`) does not exist yet, and the live database path could not find it even if it did. Shoppers therefore still read the old "traditional medicinal rice" text. After that, the remaining blockers are facts only the owner can supply: the FSSAI licence number, the full packer address, the organic certifier (or removal of the word "organic"), ingredient and nutrition panels for the drinks and mixes, a gram weight for each flower pack, and the shelf life of each product.

## The old page buried 12 px copy under 4,600 px of filler

The audit of `pages/product.html`, measured at 375×812 and 1366×900 on 13 Nov 2026, found that the page lost shoppers at the exact point where they look for product information. On a phone the page was **7,389 px tall, about 9.1 screens**. Roughly **4,600 px (62%)** of that was three related-product rows (3,216 px on their own, 43% of the page), a hard-coded blog strip and the footer. The product's own information occupied about 1,200 px of **12 px grey text** inside a five-tab box. On a 375 px screen the tab labels truncated to "Descript…" and "How to …", and the inactive tab colour measured **2.54:1** contrast against white. The light-sage tagline measured 1.93:1. A full-width rating box showed five grey stars and the word "New", saying nothing. The in-page Add to Cart landed **17 px below the 812 px fold**, so only the fixed bottom bar could sell on first paint. The discount appeared three times above the fold: as an image pill, as a price pill and in a savings line that came with an emoji. Below the tabs sat a strip of "story" circles. Tapping one threw a `TypeError`, because the modal's inner elements no longer existed, but it had already set `body.style.overflow='hidden'`. The page then stopped scrolling until it was reloaded (`product.html:4321-4382`, reproduced in the browser).

The copy itself was worse than its presentation. It had three failures. The first was **search-engine padding**. Paneer Rose's 109-word description repeats "panneer rose" eight times and offers a powder the store does not sell. One of Karunguruvai's cooking "steps" was "Can be used in Karunguruvai rice recipe in Tamil." The second was **disease and health claims**: "Controls Blood Sugar", "Diabetes Control: Maintains stable blood sugar levels", "reduces bad cholesterol", "natural remedy for constipation… helps prevent anemia", "helps improve memory". The third was **numbers and specs that contradicted each other**. Iron was given as "1.2–5 mg" under an "approximate values" banner. The facts said "Net Weight 1 kg" while 500 g was selected. Rose Milk's facts said "200 g" while its chips said ml. Karunguruvai was "hand-pounded" in the specs and "stone-milled" in the story. Every product said "Certification: FSSAI", which is a food-business licence and not an organic certificate, while the same page said "certified organic" three more times. Some images were wrong too: Rose Milk's main image was an Unsplash photo of steak and fries, and Fig Powder's was a salad bowl. On the live Mongo-id path, `mapApiProduct` dropped the database's `howToUse` and `nutritionalInfo` fields, so four of the five tabs read "not available" (`product.html:6571-6599` at audit time).

The worst content was dead code that a live product could bring back. `NS_STORIES_DATA` was the fallback for every product with no `experience.stories`, which meant every database product. It contained "After 3 months of daily kanji my blood sugar improved from 210 to 148. Doctor was amazed. — Rajan K., Chennai. 12,000+ families transformed." That is a fabricated medical testimonial, and it was served in a widget that also froze the page. Other constants contradicted the homepage and each other. They gave **152 farmers and 1,478 acres**, while the homepage says "25 acres… 200+ farming families" and the stories said "140+ farmers". They claimed a "200-point quality check at NABL-accredited partner lab" and listed ingredient claims for Moringa and Amla, which the store does not sell.

**Already done.** The five tabs are gone. In their place is a highlights list, then collapsible sections ("About this product", "How to cook" or "How to use", "Storage and shelf life") and a "Product facts" table with a note that the batch number, packing date and best-before date are printed on the pack (`product.html:4010-4033`). The sections open on screens of 900 px and wider and fold on phones, except About (`6651-6657`). All text is now HTML-escaped (`pdEsc`, `6587`), which closes the injection risk the audit raised for admin-edited text. The story circles, the `NS_*` trust, origin, comparison and ingredient blocks (now empty arrays), the hard-coded journal strip and two of the three related rows have been removed. Rose Milk and Idiyappam Flour now show a "photo coming soon" card in place of stock imagery. The working copy also carries four smaller fixes. A delivery line and a returns link driven by the admin's shipping settings now sit under the buttons (`3999-4004`, `6659`). A per-kg or per-litre price line now appears under the price (`6592-6610`). The facts table now pulls the packer name, FSSAI number and customer-care details from store settings (`6632-6646`). The savings line now reads "You save ₹X on the printed price" with no emoji (`6715`).

## One column of stacked sections beats five tabs on a phone

The new structure matches what large-sample usability research recommends, and that research also shows what the page still lacks. Baymard advises against horizontal tabs for the main product-page sections. Its test users expect vertical scrolling to reveal content, tabs scroll out of view, and participants missed tabs even while hunting for that exact information. On mobile, tabs often do not all fit on screen. Baymard recommends expanded sections with a sticky table of contents on desktop and collapsed sections on mobile, and it names unnecessary cross-sells, not content, as the real cause of long pages ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/avoid-horizontal-tabs)). Secondary summaries of earlier versions of that study put content overlooked in tabs at **27%, against 8% for collapsed sections** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/avoid-horizontal-tabs)). That figure could not be confirmed on the 2026 page. NN/g adds that accordions suit small screens but cost an interaction, because users must decide from the heading alone ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-complex-content/)). Headings therefore need to say what is inside ("How to cook: soak 3 hours, 1 : 2.5 water"), not just "Details". Leaving About open by default on phones follows Baymard's guidance that the first section can be expanded on a shorter page.

Highlights work, but the version that performs best is not a text grid. Baymard found that only **22% of sites** structure descriptions as highlights. Every test user relied on the description at some point, and users scanned the images, then the headlines, then read a paragraph if interested. The format is 2–6 features, each with a visual, a short headline and one paragraph. Reusable icons performed worse than real images but "still far outperformed text-only descriptions" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/structure-descriptions-by-highlights)). The current highlights are text with a gold left border (`product.html:3774-3777`). That is a sound start, and it keeps to the owner's rule against a templated look. The upgrade worth making later is a real photograph beside each highlight, such as the dry grain beside a coin, the cooked rice or the infusion in a glass. Generic icon sets are what make a page read as machine-made, while a photo of this farm's grain cannot be faked.

The Product facts table needs regrouping. Baymard found **50% of sites have hard-to-scan spec sheets**. The fixes are to group rows under labelled headings, put a short key-facts summary at the top (only **3% of sites** do this), and shade rows so labels and values line up ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/spec-sheet-scannability)). The current table mixes product facts and legal declarations in one list of nine rows. It should become two labelled groups. "About this rice" would hold variety, raw or parboiled, polish, grain size and colour, season and region. "Label declarations" would hold net quantity, MRP, unit price, ingredients, origin, packer, FSSAI number, best-before and customer care. The first group answers the shopper's question. The second meets the legal duty described below.

Price and buy-box rules are specific. Show the price large and bold, the original price struck through, and the saving once, grouped in the buy box ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-page-price-discounts)). The image's discount pill (`#discBadge`) still repeats the price pill and should go. Baymard's 2026 benchmark found that **81% of sites omit unit price**, **67% give no shipping estimate near the buy button** and **44% do not link the returns policy**, although 60% of shoppers look for returns information on the product page ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)). Nansei now does all three for rice, flour and drinks, which puts it ahead of most sites on these points. Dried-flower packs still show no gram weight and no unit price. For size choice, Baymard recommends buttons over drop-downs, which the store already uses. The label "Select Weight (kg)" still sits above chips reading 500 g (`product.html:6511-6513`) and should read "Pack size". Images matter too: **42% of users try to judge size from images** and 37% of sites lack an in-scale shot ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)). The gallery should therefore show the pack front, the back label, grain or petals beside a coin or spoon, and the cooked dish or brewed drink.

The fixed bottom bar on phones stays. The evidence for sticky add-to-cart bars is thin: one vendor's single-site A/B test found an **8.7% lift** in add-to-cart rate ([CleanCommit](https://cleancommit.io/ab-tests/mobile-bottom-sticky-add-to-cart-button/)), and Baymard's own findings on the subject are paywalled. There is a cheaper way to get the in-page button above the fold. The empty rating row takes about 80 px on a phone, and hiding it until a product has real reviews should lift the in-page Add to Cart above the 812 px fold. That figure is inferred from the audit measurements and has not been re-measured.

## Indian law turns the facts table into a legal declaration

Two regimes decide what must be on the page before a shopper pays. Under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011, rule 6(10) as inserted in 2017, an e-commerce seller must show the package declarations on the listing ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/662702/e-commerce-entities-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2017)). Those declarations are the name and address of the manufacturer or packer, generic name, net quantity, **MRP inclusive of all taxes**, unit sale price, consumer care details and country of origin, plus date declarations ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/compliances-for-website-e-commerce-website/)). Under the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations 2020, reg 4(2), all mandatory label information must be given before sale online, except the batch number and date markings ([TaxGuru reproduction](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/food-safety-standards-labelling-display-regulations-2020.html)). The mandatory items are the name of the food, ingredients, nutrition per 100 g or ml, the veg symbol, additives, the FSSAI logo and licence number, the full address, instructions for use and an allergen declaration. Single-ingredient unprocessed foods are exempt from the nutrition panel under reg 5(3)(c), and certified organic food must carry the organic logo (same source). FSSAI's December 2024 advisory adds that food delivered online must have **at least 30% of its shelf life, or 45 days, remaining**. It also requires online claims to match the physical label ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/fssai-issues-advisory-for-e-commerce-food-business-operators-to-ensure-consumer-protection-279669); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/02/24/india-tightens-food-safety-standards-for-e-commerce-platforms/)). The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 also require a named grievance officer, with acknowledgement of complaints within 48 hours ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/guides/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)). That obligation sits at site level, in the footer or the contact page, rather than on each product.

The sources conflict on dates. The Legal Metrology list includes date of manufacture and best-before, while FSSAI exempts date marking online. The safe course is the one the new page already takes: state the shelf-life policy and say that the exact date is printed on the pack. The official gazette text of rule 6(10) was not retrieved, so the precise list and the required unit-price format (per kg, or per 100 g for small packs) remain unconfirmed. Have a food-regulatory adviser check the final block.

One live line conflicts with the rules. Under the price the page now says "5% GST is added at checkout" (`product.html:6605`). The rules require the MRP to be inclusive of all taxes. A price shown before tax, with tax added later, is also the "extra costs" problem that **40% of abandoning shoppers** cite ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). The fix is to show the tax-inclusive price on the product page, labelled "Inclusive of all taxes", and to make checkout show GST as a breakdown inside that total rather than an addition. This is a pricing decision for the owner and their accountant, but the current wording should not go live as it is.

## Disease claims are banned outright, and the old copy made them in every benefit list

The FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations 2018 cover websites. Reg 2 defines advertising to include the "internet or website" ([FSSAI compendium](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Advertising_Claims_Regulations_16_11_2021.pdf)). Reg 10(1) bars any claim that a food is suitable for the "prevention, alleviation, treatment or cure of a disease, disorder or particular physiological condition" unless another regulation permits it. Reg 10(2) bars any suggestion that a food is recommended or approved by doctors. Reg 4 requires claims to be "truthful, unambiguous, meaningful, not misleading" (same source). Deceptive claims are punishable under Section 53 of the FSS Act ([FSSAI Digest](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php)). That rules out every benefit list the old page carried: "Controls Blood Sugar", "Diabetes Control", "Boosts Immunity", "helps in weight loss", "remedy for constipation", "prevents anemia", "improves memory", "reduces stress". It also rules out the folk beliefs that the research sources record about these products: Karunguruvai for filariasis, Karuppu Kavuni for dog bites, Mappillai Samba for virility, Iluppai Poo Samba for paralysis and joint pain, Arubatham Kuruvai for anaemia, avarampoo for diabetes and rose for kidneys and menstruation. Even government training material and the CIKS seed-bank book repeat these beliefs ([CIKS source book](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [NIFTEM rose processing](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)). A credible source does not make a disease claim legal.

"Low GI" and "diabetic-friendly" fail on two counts. Schedule I has no glycaemic-index criterion, so the claim falls under the health-claim rules, which need substantiation and FSSAI approval (compendium, regs 7 and 11–12). The only GI data for these rices is a single CIKS-commissioned study that used **White Ponni rice as the reference (=100), not glucose**. It found 53.81 for Karunguruvai and 68.84 for Mappillai Samba ([CIKS Table 8](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)). Neither number means "low GI" on the scale shoppers know. Competitors such as Two Brothers sell "Low GI" rice ([Two Brothers](https://twobrothersindiashop.com/products/low-gi-rice-kalanamak-variety)), and Jivabhumi writes "supports digestion, heart health" ([Jivabhumi](https://www.jivabhumi.com/products/mapillai-samba-rice-unpolished)). That suggests weak enforcement, not legality. Nansei gains nothing by copying them and keeps the exposure if it does.

Nutrient adjectives have numeric thresholds, and the old copy fails them. A "source of fibre" claim needs **at least 3 g per 100 g**, and "high fibre" needs at least 6 g (compendium, Schedule I). The only lab figure for Karunguruvai is **1.92 g dietary fibre per 100 g** ([CIKS Table 9](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)), so the old "high fiber content" line was false against the best available evidence. Mappillai Samba measured 1.79 g and Iluppai Poo Samba below 1 g. Karuppu Kavuni measured 3.64 g, but that is one 2019 sample from another farm. No claim should rest on it without Nansei's own analysis. Dried figs average about 9.8 g fibre per 100 g in USDA data ([USDA mirror](https://nutrientnorms.pages.dev/foods/figs/)). A fig powder could therefore qualify for "high fibre", but only on a test of the actual powder.

Several words carry legal conditions in Schedule V. "Natural" requires a single food with nothing added and only minimal processing. "Pure" requires a single ingredient with nothing added. "Real", "authentic" and "genuine" are allowed only if the page explains how the quality is tangibly justified. "Traditional" requires a formulation or method unchanged for **30 years** (compendium). Heritage rice clearly meets "traditional", so "a traditional Tamil Nadu variety" is safe. "Traditional medicinal rice" is not, because "medicinal" is the problem. Three product names need a decision. "Organic Real Rose Milk" carries "organic", which requires NPOP or PGS-India certification and the Jaivik Bharat logo ([FSSAI](https://fssai.gov.in/cms/organic-food.php)), and "Real", which needs an ingredient list showing rose extract rather than flavouring. "Black Urad Dal Health Mix Powder" implies a health benefit in the name. "Mani Samba", the store's alias for Mappillai Samba, was not found in any source. Small producers with turnover under ₹50 lakh who sell direct are exempt from organic certification but may not use the logo ([FSSAI clipping, 2019](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_CP_Krishi_21_06_2019.pdf)). If that exemption is Nansei's position, the page can say "grown without synthetic pesticides on our farm" only if it is true, but not "certified organic".

Some honest wording cuts the other way. For three products the evidence supports a neutral caution, and the owner should decide whether to show one. In rats, avaram flower tea raised levels of the drugs theophylline and carbamazepine, and an extract cut metformin absorption by 60% ([Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/)). Flower extract of *Hibiscus rosa-sinensis* caused antifertility effects in male rats, and human data are "essentially absent" ([Raza et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/)). EFSA raised safety objections in 2022 to dried butterfly pea flowers as a traditional food ([EFSA](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/sp.efsa.2022.EN-7084)), although the US permits the extract as a colour additive ([21 CFR 73.69](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/73.69)). A line such as "If you are pregnant or take regular medication, ask your doctor before drinking flower infusions" is a caution, not a claim. FSSAI's status for these dried flowers as foods was not found, and that question belongs with the regulatory adviser.

## Every one of the 16 products has a few honest facts and an owner gap

The writing pattern that holds up legally is also the one that reads as human. NN/g's rewrite study found that removing promotional language alone improved usability by 27%, and that concise, scannable and objective copy together improved it by 124% ([NN/g, 1997](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)). Baymard's DTC research found that shoppers want specific ingredients, origin and production practices, and that they dismiss products that lack specifics ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/dtc-users-informational-needs)). Jivabhumi's Mapillai Samba page is the best local model for tone. It describes a "reddish-brown hue, and firm texture", grains that "hold shape after cooking", dish pairings of biryani and pongal, and numbered steps: soak 2–3 hours, 4–5 whistles, natural release ([Jivabhumi](https://www.jivabhumi.com/products/mapillai-samba-rice-unpolished)). For Nansei that becomes a fixed shape per category.

For rice, the About section has three short paragraphs. The first is the fact line: variety, season, grain, raw or parboiled. The second is what it looks and cooks like. The third is the dishes Tamil households cook with it, told as culture rather than effect. How to cook gives rinse, soak, water ratio, whistles or open-pot minutes, rest time and one dish. For dried flowers: the plant and the part used, the colour of the infusion, how to brew, and what it is used in. For flours: which rice, raw or roasted, what it is for, the dough method, and a shelf life that is shorter than whole grain. For drinks: the full ingredient list, the dilution and serving method from the label, and "refrigerate after opening". Every sentence should be one a shopper could check against the pack or a cookbook. Phrases like "nature's goodness", "superfood", "elevate your meals" and "nourish body and soul" should go, along with stacked adjectives, emoji and unverifiable superlatives.

A model About text for Karunguruvai, using only sourced facts, would read: "A dark traditional Tamil Nadu rice, grown in the Kuruvai and Navarai seasons. The husk is black and the parboiled grain is dark brown and bold. It cooks firm and fluffy rather than sticky, and holds its shape. In Tamil homes it is used for idli and dosa batter as well as everyday rice." The How to cook section would read: "Soak 3 hours. Use 2½ cups of water to 1 cup of rice. Pressure-cook for 4 whistles." Each element traces to the CIKS profile and cooking sheet, plus a 2024 cooking-quality study ([CIKS](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149)). These are a seed bank's descriptions. The owner must confirm that Nansei's lot matches them, for example that it is parboiled, before the text goes live.

## The copy file is missing, and the live path could not find it anyway

The new sections read their text from `window.PRODUCT_COPY` in `js/product-copy.js`, keyed by product number (`product.html:6584-6591`). **That file does not exist and the page does not load it.** `renderDetails` therefore falls back to the old `p.desc` (`6619`). For the in-page catalogue, that is the "traditional medicinal rice… known for its low glycemic index and high fiber content" paragraph (`5023`). The live path has a second break. When a shopper arrives from the homepage with a Mongo id, `loadProduct` replaces the product with `mapApiProduct(data.product)` (`6428`). That mapper does not copy `legacyId` (`6388-6416`), although the backend model and `js/catalog.js` both carry it. `pdCopy` looks up `p.legacyId || p.id`, and on this path `p.id` is the Mongo `_id`, so the lookup fails. Even with the copy file written, production pages would still show the database's old description. The fix takes two lines: map `legacyId` in `mapApiProduct` (or reuse `catalog.js`), and add the script tag. Writing the 16 copy entries is the actual work, and the table above gives the source for each.

## Conclusion

On this page the legal constraint and the design constraint point the same way. FSSAI bars the benefit language, Legal Metrology requires the facts table, and the "does not look AI-generated" requirement is met by the same thing both laws push toward: specific, checkable statements about one farm's grain. The best evidence available, the CIKS lab sheet, quietly disproves the old "high fibre" line. That shows how much of the original copy was written from habit rather than knowledge. The honest page will also be shorter than the old one, and Baymard's finding that tabs fail while scrolling does not supports that choice. A shorter page means fewer screens of cross-sell and no repeated discount.

The redesign is now limited by content, not code. The structure is in place, but until the copy file exists and the API path can reach it, every live product page still serves the old medicinal text inside the new sections. A blocker for honest copy on ten of the sixteen products is a supplier answer the owner already has, or can get with one phone call. The practical sequence is to wire the copy file, ship the five documented rices first with the owner's confirmation, and hold the flowers, drinks, flour and mix until their labels, species and weights are in hand. Shipping placeholder text for those ten would undo the cleanup.
