# Fix the plumbing, keep the storefront's look

The restored Jul 23 Nansei storefront mostly needs repairs, not a redesign. Its worst problems are invisible ones. The checkout script failed to parse in the committed build. Every order payload sends the pincode "000000", which the backend rejects. A customer who pays online can be left with no confirmation and a full cart. On the product page, one click adds two units, a 500 g rice pack can enter the cart at ₹90,000, and dried-flower pages show prices about 50 times too high. Almost all of these are code and data fixes with no visible effect. The second layer is content that reads as placeholder or machine-made. Examples are the "+91 99999 99999" phone number, a Rick Roll video in the mill section, a black empty reels box floating over the product rows, a countdown that resets every midnight, hard-coded star ratings, "?" where ₹ and → should be, and claims that contradict each other from section to section. Replacing those with real, specific, checkable detail does more against the "AI-generated" impression than any visual change, and it leaves the forest-green, gold and cream design untouched. Third come the India-specific purchase blockers: login forced at the Pay button, GST added on top of shown prices, no delivery or COD information on the product page, and no FSSAI licence number. These are the friction points that large studies tie to abandoned carts ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). Only a short list of items, mainly a pause control for the auto-moving hero and gold-text contrast, would visibly change the design. They are marked so the owner can decide.

**How to read the tags.** **Invisible** means behaviour, data, copy, markup or performance changes with no visible design change. Restoring a corrupted glyph, such as ₹ in place of "?", counts as invisible because it brings back the intended text. **Small addition** means a new line, link, field or page built in the existing component styles. **Visible design change** means the look itself shifts, and those items need the owner's sign-off. Line references come from the audit notes and point to the current files in `pages/`.

## The checkout crashed on load, and the product page misprices every order

The most important finding is that the shopping flow fails at three points between "Add to Cart" and a placed order. The checkout page's main script threw `SyntaxError: Identifier 'reviewBtns' has already been declared` in the committed build. That left the cart showing "0 items · ₹0", with no working buttons and a dead My Orders view. The duplicate `let reviewBtns = ''` sat at `checkoutmyorderpage.html:2144`, it came back with the Jul 23 restore, and it is absent from the October build 479b6a4. The uncommitted working-tree change to that file deletes this line. Running `node --check` on both inline script blocks of the working copy now passes, while the committed HEAD still fails at that line. The audit notes also describe a second, hidden error (a missing comma near lines 2108–2109). That error did not reproduce: the products mapping line ends with a comma in both HEAD and the working tree. **The P0 action here is to commit the existing one-line fix and reload the page to confirm.**

Behind the parse error sit defects that would block or corrupt real orders. The saved address line is built as `City  600001` with two spaces (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:1682`). The parser that reads it back expects a hyphen (`1880-1888`), so **every order payload carries pincode "000000"** and the hard-coded state "Tamil Nadu" (`1943-1952`). The backend's `cleanAddress` rejects that pincode. After a successful Razorpay payment, the page POSTs `/orders` a second time, and the current backend refuses this because only COD is accepted on that route (`backend/routes/orderRoutes.js:334`). The fallback `_saveLocalOrder` then reads `isCod`, a variable local to another function (`1969-1971`, `1994`), and throws a ReferenceError. The customer has paid, but sees no success screen and the cart is not cleared. The page also has three false-success payment paths: a failed signature check is ignored (`1907-1913`), a Razorpay constructor error is treated as "Simulate success for demo" (`1862-1868`), and orders carry `isPaid:true` with a fabricated payment id (`1954-1956`). All of these are invisible fixes. Store pincode, city and state as separate fields. After a successful verify, show the success screen from that response and skip the second POST. Delete the simulated paths.

The product page's cart logic is broken in ways a shopper sees immediately once they reach checkout. `addToCartFn()` writes the item and then calls `Cart.add(cartItem, 0)`, which adds it again, so **one click gives quantity 2** (`product.html:7632-7636`). The displayed price uses the first variant (₹90 for 500 g) while the cart stores 0.25 kg at ₹45 (`7516-7519`, `7312`). Clicking the "500 g" button computes `parseFloat("500g") × ₹180` = **₹90,000**, with the label "500g kg" (`7307`, `7132-7136`). Dried-flower pages run the variant pricing even though the variant buttons are hidden, so Paneer Rose shows **₹7,500** while the cart receives ₹150 (`7288`, `7587-7604`). The cart merges lines by product id only, so a 1 kg pack increments a 500 g line (`7633-7634`). The wishlist page already merges on `id + qtyLabel` (`wishlist.html:701`), which is the pattern to copy. "Buy Now" overwrites the whole cart with one item (`product.html:7667`). The mobile sticky-bar stepper always calls the flower function, so its number never updates (`4615-4640`, `7533-7537`). Finally, `product.html:10` sets `window.API_BASE = ''` and doesn't load `js/config.js`, so on the live site every API call falls back to `localhost:5000`. Real products, reviews and the review form therefore never load in production. **These are all invisible fixes.** The root cause is three separate cart implementations with different item shapes (`product.html:26-31`, `wishlist.html:697`, `checkoutmyorderpage.html:2386`). One shared helper with a single price function removes the whole class of bug.

My Orders has the same pattern of things that look finished but don't work. For signed-in users, Track, Invoice and Buy Again look up local storage instead of the API orders that were rendered, and they throw (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:2193-2215`, `2366-2387`). Cancel only edits local storage, even though `PUT /api/orders/:id/cancel` exists. Return posts nothing and still says "Return request submitted. We'll contact you within 24h" (`2354-2385`). The WhatsApp receipt is built after the cart is cleared, so it reads Rs.0 (`1977`, `2030-2075`). A false confirmation does more damage than a missing feature, because the customer believes something happened.

## Placeholder and fabricated content is what reads as machine-made

The notes contain no study of what makes a site "look AI-generated", so this section is an inference from the audit evidence. The design itself is distinctive: a serif headline, a cinematic video hero and a coherent green, gold and cream palette. What feels generic or fake is the content inside it. Placeholders made it to production. The footer lists "+91 99999 99999" and `info@nansaiorganics.com`, an email domain that doesn't match the site's `nanseiorg.in` (`index.html:2856-2857`, `product.html:5682-5750`, `js/config.js:20`). Two mill-section cards and every product-page fallback video point to YouTube id `dQw4w9WgXcQ`, the "Never Gonna Give You Up" placeholder. Other video URLs in that section are truncated (`embXcQ`, `eXcQ`) or use a mistyped ImageKit bucket (`index.html:2479-2566`, `product.html:4431`, `5136-5141`). All four reels have `src:''`, so the floating widget is a black 195×347 px box that auto-advances over the product cards (`index.html:3727-3731`). Hero slides 2 and 3 have `background-image:url('')` and show as near-black panels (`index.html:1315`, `1326`). The gallery's lightbox markup was replaced by a comment, so tile clicks do nothing and throw at `index.html:4068` (`1630`, `4056-4071`). `video-reels.html` is titled "Video Reels Component" and plays Big Buck Bunny (`video-reels.html:6`, `325-340`).

Fabricated precision adds to the impression. The offer-bar countdown always counts down to 23:59:59 today (`index.html:2899-2907`), so it is an evergreen false-urgency timer. "Paneer Rose Only 32 Left" is hard-coded text (`1140`, `2888`). Static cards carry invented ratings such as "4.7 (142)" (`2936-2952`). The API fallback invents an old price as `price*1.25`, so every product always looks discounted (`3493`). A product with zero reviews still shows hard-coded bars for 3★ 8, 2★ 4 and 1★ 3 (`product.html:7408-7412`). The business claims contradict each other across sections: "22 years" against "25+ years" against "over a decade" (`index.html:1319`, `2649`, `2666`); "140+ farmers", "200 families", "thousands" and "Loved by 12,000+ Families" (`product.html:4729`, `4891-4897`); "USDA Organic" and an awards headline in the newsroom against "NPOP & FSSAI" on the product page (`index.html:2672`, `news.html:1206-1230`). The brand is spelled Nansai, NANSAI, Nansei and NANSEI across titles, alt text, coupon codes and the copyright line (`index.html:7`, `1187`, `2856`). Developer wording leaks into the shopper's view: "Forgot Password placeholder - reset email flow is ready for backend email delivery" (`login.html:625-634`), "Is the backend running?" (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:1916-1927`), and a Refer & Earn card offering "₹100 store credits" with code "NSI-TESTUS" when no referral logic exists in the backend. Encoding damage turned →, ₹, — and emoji into literal "?" on **118 source lines** of `index.html`. Shoppers see "Explore Products ?" and "Free Delivery on Orders Above ?999" (`1136`, `1297`, `1311`, `2677`, `3726`), and the Pay button reads "Pay Now ?504" (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:1430-1432`, `1727-1729`).

The fix in every case is real, specific detail, and that is also what strong Indian organic brands lead with. Two Brothers Organic Farms puts a "Good Food Backed by Proof" block, lab reports and farm-to-fork traceability on its homepage, though only 29 of its 100+ products have dynamic traceability so far ([TBOF](https://twobrothersindiashop.com/); [TBOF traceability](https://twobrothersindiashop.com/pages/traceability)). Organic Tattva shows certification logos and recipe content tied to its products ([Organic Tattva](https://www.organictattva.com/)). Both show star ratings on cards. Nansei should show ratings only once real reviews exist. For a food seller, specificity is also a compliance matter. FSSAI's December 2024 advisory to e-commerce food businesses points to displaying the seller's FSSAI licence number and clear images of the label panel showing batch, expiry and MRP ([FSSAI advisory](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2024/12/674efa161d756Adobe%20Scan%203%20Dec%202024.pdf); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/02/24/india-tightens-food-safety-standards-for-e-commerce-platforms/)). Secondary summaries differ on whether the licence display is mandatory or encouraged. Under NPOP rules, "organic" requires at least 95% certified ingredients, and labels should name the accredited certifier ([APEDA SOP](https://jaivikbharat.fssai.gov.in/SOP_for_FSO.pdf)). The status of the Jaivik Bharat logo depends on gazette notification and should be checked before the logo is used ([FSSAI Jaivik Bharat](https://jaivikbharat.fssai.gov.in/)). The unverified "USDA Organic", "9 certifications", "24/7 Support" and award claims are therefore a legal exposure as well as a trust problem. **Replace them with the actual certifier name and number, or remove them.**

## Indian shoppers abandon on surprise costs and forced login, and the site does both

Baymard's abandonment data comes from US shoppers, but its leading causes map directly onto this checkout. **Extra costs were cited by 40%**, distrust of the site with card details by 19%, forced account creation by 18%, a long checkout by 17%, site errors by 17%, and being unable to see the total up front by 12% ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). Nansei's checkout adds **5% GST on top** of the prices shown on the product page and in the cart. Step 2 omits the GST row, so the figures on screen don't add up: Subtotal ₹480, Discount −₹120, Total ₹504 (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:1391-1397`, `1501-1507`). Every cart line says "Free Delivery", while the trust pill says free delivery applies only above ₹999, and the backend charges `SHIPPING_FEE` below that threshold (`1345`, `945`, `backend/utils/orderGuard.js:9-10`). Guests can fill the cart and address and reach Payment. Only at "Pay Now" are they told to log in and redirected to `login.html` without a return URL (`1767`), and login always sends them to the homepage (`login.html:600-601`, `621-622`). That is the 18% account-creation problem placed at the worst possible moment.

The product page gives no delivery, returns or COD information. A trust block covering 2–5 day delivery, UPI/cards/COD, 7-day returns and NPOP/FSSAI is already written as `NS_TRUST_DATA` (`product.html:5045-5056`), but its container `#nsTrustGrid` was never placed in the markup. Adding that one container renders existing copy in an existing card style. Baymard found that **64% of users looked for shipping costs before adding to cart**, and that even a price range beats showing nothing ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/show-shipping-costs-on-product-pages)). Shoppers also prefer a delivery date to a transit window, but they treat a displayed date as a firm promise ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/shipping-speed-vs-delivery-date)). A pincode check showing a date is therefore worth adding only if the courier's estimates are reliable. The confirmation screen's current "+5 days" date is invented on the client (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:1933-1934`).

Payment mix matters more in India than the US data suggests. UPI made up **85.5% of payment transactions by volume** in H2 2025 ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?Id=23813)). Yet prepaid was only **41% of e-commerce orders in 2025**, up from 32% in 2024, and just 34.5% in Tier III cities ([Social Samosa/ClickPost](https://www.socialsamosa.com/report/prepaid-e-commerce-orders-rise-10958917)). COD carries the risk: D2C COD orders are returned to origin **37.51% of the time against 5.28% for prepaid** ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/ecommerce-rto-statistics-india)), and return rates climb with slower first delivery attempts ([Cargo Breaking News/Shipway](https://cargobreakingnews.com/?p=34465)). The checkout already offers Razorpay (UPI first) and COD, which is the right base. The cheap additions are making prepaid the default, confirming COD orders over WhatsApp before dispatch, and showing any COD fee or prepaid discount in the total. None of the notes measured the effect of these tactics for food and grocery specifically, so treat them as standard practice rather than proven uplift. The address form is otherwise good: 10-digit mobile validation and PIN-to-city/state autofill are already in place (`1589-1700`). It needs `inputmode="numeric"` on the PIN and mobile fields, and an editable State field for when the postal API is unreachable (`1650`, `1665-1668`).

## Most performance, accessibility and SEO wins cannot be seen

On a mid-range phone, media is the heaviest cost. The hero autoplays a **9.57 MB, 60-second video with no poster**, the same file is referenced twice (`index.html:1291`, `1305`), and slide 0 doesn't advance until the video ends (`3396-3408`). ImageKit can serve a 60 KB thumbnail frame as a poster. A re-encode to a short loop of 1–2 MB, plus `preload="none"` on the second copy, changes nothing visible. Product-card images ship at 1080–3375 px for 166 px slots. With `?tr=w-360` appended, the Fig Powder image drops from 299 KB to **15.6 KB (−95%)**, and roughly 1.4 MB comes off the home page in total ([ImageKit resize docs](https://imagekit.io/docs/image-resize-and-crop)). None of the 55 images have `width`/`height` attributes, which web.dev recommends to prevent layout shift ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/browser-level-image-lazy-loading)). Every page blocks render on the Tailwind Play CDN (126 KB), which Tailwind says is "not intended for production" ([Tailwind docs](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/installation/play-cdn)), and on unpinned `lucide@latest`. The unpinned Lucide has already removed the Instagram icon from the product page. The home page has **2,452 DOM elements**, well over Lighthouse's roughly 1,400-node error level ([Chrome Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/dom-size)), because every product renders twice and there are eight sourceless video elements. The evidence that speed sells is older and not India-specific: Google and Deloitte associated a 0.1 s mobile speed gain with 8.4% more retail conversions ([web.dev case study](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)). The practical targets are LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Real field LCP was not measured, so run PageSpeed Insights on the Netlify URL before and after.

Accessibility work is almost entirely invisible. The home page has 56 icon buttons with no accessible name (wishlist hearts, slider dots, arrows, search, and a hamburger with no `aria-expanded`), and the product page has 30. WCAG 4.1.2 requires each control to have a name and role ([WCAG 4.1.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/name-role-value.html)). Checkout's visible labels aren't tied to their inputs: 23 of 24 controls have no programmatic label, starting at `checkoutmyorderpage.html:565-566`. There are 40 click-only `div`/`span` elements and no `<main>` or skip link ([WCAG 2.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/bypass-blocks.html)). The mobile drawer neither moves focus nor closes on Escape (`index.html:3568-3576`). A global arrow-key handler changes hero slides while the shopper types in search (`3425`). Tap targets are small: the hero dots are 7×7 px, the hamburger 28×22, header icons 32×32 (`index.html:469`), and every Add to Cart button 146×26. A transparent `::before` hit area enlarges most of them without changing how they look. WCAG 2.2's 24 px AA minimum is the floor ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). SEO basics are absent on all 11 pages: no meta description, Open Graph tags, canonical, favicon or Product JSON-LD. There is no robots.txt or sitemap, and the `/*` catch-all in `netlify.toml` returns the homepage with status 200 for every missing URL, a soft 404. Google recommends putting Product markup in the initial HTML ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet)).

Two accessibility failures cannot be fixed without a visible change, and they are the main exceptions to "don't change the look". The hero auto-advances every 5 s, the reels every 6 s, and the marquee runs forever, with no pause control. WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires a way to pause content like this ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). A small pause icon styled like the existing `#slide0-sound-btn` would satisfy it with minimal visual impact. Gold text on light backgrounds measures **2.08–2.92:1** against a 4.5:1 requirement ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). This affects the small eyebrows and the checkout totals (`checkoutmyorderpage.html:682`, `853`, `936`). Gold on forest green passes at 7.28:1, so keeping gold on dark backgrounds and using #7a5f17 or forest-deep text on cream stays inside the palette. It is still a visible shade shift.

## What the owner must decide, and what stayed unverified

A few items are business calls rather than fixes. The founder section ("Meet the Farmer") is back on the page after the owner earlier asked for its removal. Its first paragraph also starts with a blank where the name was taken out (`index.html:2624-2660`, `2645`). Decide whether it stays. If it stays, repair the sentence. Other owner decisions: whether dried flowers sell by grams or pieces (`product.html:7576-7578`), which real phone number and email belong in the footer, whether to keep showing every product twice (in New/Best Sellers and again in its category row), and which certification and history numbers are true.

Several gaps remain open. No backend was running during the audit, so real Razorpay, COD and API order flows are code-inferred. That includes whether the backend would accept a ₹90,000 line price for a static product id. There are no field LCP or INP numbers, no screen-reader pass, and the contrast checker treated image or gradient backgrounds as white, which makes some failures probable false positives. Every Baymard figure comes from US shoppers. The notes found no India-specific abandonment survey and no measured uplift for food D2C from pincode checks, WhatsApp COD confirmation or Tamil-language UI.

## Conclusion

The site does not look AI-generated because of its design. It reads that way because a polished shell surrounds hollow content: placeholder numbers, demo videos, invented ratings and timers, and claims that can't all be true. The constraint "don't change the look" is therefore less limiting than it sounds. Most of the work, and nearly all of the value, is invisible: making checkout actually place orders, making the product-page price match the cart, and swapping every stand-in for a verifiable fact. A heritage-rice brand can lead with the village, the harvest month, the certifier's number and the FSSAI licence, and generic templates cannot fake those details.

The sequencing matters. Fix the order path before adding anything. A pincode checker or a trust block on a product page whose cart line is ₹90,000 adds polish to a broken sale. The deeper cause is three copies of cart logic, a header, a footer and a catalogue duplicated on each page, and an encoding round-trip that ate the ₹ sign. Consolidating the cart helper and switching special characters to HTML entities are the two structural changes that stop these bugs from coming back after the next restore.
