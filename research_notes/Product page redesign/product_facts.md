# Product fact sheets for the 16 Nansei Organics products (sourced, no health claims)

Notes for the writer
- "Source quality" key: [P] = peer-reviewed or government/regulator document; [NGO] = CIKS (Centre for Indian Knowledge Systems), the main NGO conserving Tamil traditional rice; it publishes documentation, not peer-reviewed work; [S] = secondary (Wikipedia, regulatory-news sites, nutrient-database mirrors); [W] = weak (blogs or sellers), shown only to flag a claim or a conflict.
- CIKS source book = *Traditional Rice Varieties of Tamil Nadu: A Source Book*, CIKS, Chennai (authors A.V. Balasubramanian, K. Vijayalakshmi, Subhashini Sridhar and others; the upload is dated 2020, which is inferred from the URL). Its Table 9 nutrient analyses were carried out by the NABL-accredited lab of National Agro Foundation, Chennai, in 2019, on 49 varieties. Values are per 100 g of uncooked grain. "Boiled" in that table means parboiled rice and "Raw" means raw (pachai) rice. — [CIKS source book](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- These are single-sample analyses of one lot. Grain from another farm or season will differ, so any number shown on a page should be worded as "a 2019 CIKS lab analysis found…" and not as a guaranteed label value.
- IFCT 2017 (NIN) could not be retrieved: the PDF at nin.res.in is over 10 MB and the fetch tool refused it. No IFCT numbers are quoted below. See Gaps.

## Cross-cutting: what Indian food law allows on these pages

### Takeaway
FSSAI rules bar food sellers from claiming a food prevents, treats or cures any disease. "Organic" on the label legally requires NPOP or PGS-India certification and the Jaivik Bharat logo, apart from a small-producer exemption. Product pages should describe origin, appearance, taste, cooking and measured composition only.

### Cited Findings
- The FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations 2018, final version published 19 Nov 2018, prohibit claims that a food is suitable for preventing, alleviating, treating or curing a disease, disorder or physiological condition, unless another regulation specifically permits it. Reduction-of-disease-risk claims are allowed only for specified risk factors or with prior FSSAI approval. — [FSSAI Advertising & Claims compendium](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Advertising_Claims_Regulations_16_11_2021.pdf); [FSSAI Digest on claims](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php) [P; the exact wording was read through secondary summaries, so check the gazette text]
- Deceptive claims are punishable under Section 53 of the FSS Act 2006. — [FSSAI Digest](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php) [P/S]
- Under the FSS (Organic Foods) Regulations 2017, food sold as "organic" must meet NPOP or PGS-India requirements and carry the Jaivik Bharat logo. Foods still in conversion cannot use the logo. — [FSSAI organic food page](https://fssai.gov.in/cms/organic-food.php) [P]; [Consumer Voice](https://consumer-voice.org/?p=49972) [S]
- Small producers with turnover under Rs 50 lakh a year who sell direct to consumers were exempted from certification, but they may not use the Jaivik Bharat logo. — [FSSAI news clipping, Jun 2019](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_CP_Krishi_21_06_2019.pdf) [S]
- Sources disagree on when the organic rules took effect: 1 Jul 2018, 1 Jan 2019 or 1 Apr 2019. — [FSSAI news PDF](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/5a9e865ea8b0cFSSAI_News_Organic_Food_FNB_23_01_2018.pdf); [Consumer Voice](https://consumer-voice.org/?p=49972)

### Inferences
- The product named "Organic Real Rose Milk" and any other page that says "organic" need the certification and logo status confirmed with the owner before the word stays.
- Several sources used below, including the CIKS book, the AEER review and a NIFTEM/PMFME training deck, repeat traditional medicinal beliefs. Quoting them on a product page would still be a disease claim. They are recorded here only so the writer knows what to leave out.

### Gaps
- I did not read the gazette text of either regulation directly. The wording above comes from FSSAI compendium and digest summaries.

## 1. Karunguruvai Rice (Karunkuruvai, கருங்குறுவை)

### Takeaway
A dark traditional Tamil Nadu paddy: the grain (husk) is black and the rice is dark brown and bold. It is grown in the Kuruvai and Navarai seasons and is usually sold parboiled. It is used for idli, dosa and everyday table rice. The only cooking guidance found is the CIKS sheet: soak 3 h, rice to water 1:2.5, 4 pressure-cooker whistles.

### Cited Findings
- **Identity and agronomy:** Navarai and Kuruvai seasons; 125-day crop; average height 95 cm. "Grain is black in colour, rice is dark brown in colour, bold rice"; 1,000-grain weight 25.48 g. — [CIKS source book, variety profile 14](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO]
- **Name:** The CIKS book lists the variety in its Tamil-names table and calls Karunkuruvai a "dark variety", in a section on historic Tamil rice names. The name literally reads *karu* (black) + *kuruvai* (the short June-to-September season). That etymology is my inference and is consistent with the CIKS season data. — [CIKS source book](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Dishes:** "It is ideal for making idly, dosa." — [CIKS profile 14](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Cooking:** CIKS "Luxury Collection" consumer sheet: rice to water 1:2.5, 4 whistles, coarse texture, soak 3 hours before cooking. The PDF's table columns are partly misaligned, but the ratio, whistles and soak lines read cleanly for this row. — [CIKS source book, cooking-instructions table](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO]
- **Texture (lab):** Intermediate amylose (23%), so it should cook "firm and fluffy". It showed the highest breadth-wise expansion on cooking of the nine varieties tested. Cooked length 9.5 mm against 5.60 mm raw; water absorption ratio 3.75 g/g. — [Jency & Vishnuprabha 2024, Plant Archives 24(1):1075-1082](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149) [P]
- **Grain size (lab):** Kernel length 5.60 mm, breadth 2.02 mm, L/B 2.78 (medium). — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149)
- **Composition (CIKS 2019, parboiled, per 100 g uncooked):** moisture 11.84 g; ash 1.54 g; protein 8.22 g; fat 1.91 g; dietary fibre 1.92 g; carbohydrate 74.57 g; energy 348.35 kcal; potassium 249.70 mg; iron 7.60 mg; calcium 37.70 mg; magnesium 94.70 mg; zinc 2.80 mg; phosphorus 344.5 mg. — [CIKS source book Table 9, row 14](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO; single sample]
- **GI study:** CIKS reports a GI of 53.81 with White Ponni rice as the control (=100), not glucose. — [CIKS source book Table 8](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Conflict:** A seller blog gives 120–150 days and "short grain". — [salemmango.com](https://www.salemmango.com/blog/karun-kuruvai-rice-online-repair-cell-damage) [W]. Use the CIKS figure of 125 days.

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Cures filariasis" or "used by Siddha physicians to treat filariasis": CIKS records this as a belief, and the AEER review lists it as a "cure". — [CIKS profile 14](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [Anuratha et al. 2025, AEER 23(1):325-342](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). It is a disease-treatment claim with no clinical evidence, so it is barred by the FSSAI 2018 rules.
- "Good for diabetics", "most suited for diabetic patients" or "low GI": the GI result comes from one CIKS-commissioned study against White Ponni rice, and "for diabetics" is a disease claim. — [CIKS Table 8](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- "Wards off skin diseases or urinary problems": historic text, quoted in the CIKS book, about a disease. — [CIKS source book](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- "Repairs cell damage": seller copy. — [salemmango.com](https://www.salemmango.com/blog/karun-kuruvai-rice-online-repair-cell-damage) [W]

### Inferences
- A safe line would be: "Dark-brown, bold parboiled grain; soak about 3 hours and cook at 1:2.5. It holds a firm grain, good for idli and dosa batters as well as table rice."

### Gaps
- No independent shelf-life data. General storage advice (airtight, cool, dry) is common practice, but I found no source specific to this variety.

## 2. Karuppu Kavuni (Black Kavuni Rice, கருப்பு கவுனி)

### Takeaway
A black-pericarp Tamil rice historically linked to the Chettiar community, who serve it on special occasions. Its best-documented use is in sweets and desserts, and also idli and dosa. Two peer-reviewed studies found it the highest in phenolics of the Kavuni types tested, and among the highest in the other varieties they compared. Its crop duration is reported inconsistently.

### Cited Findings
- **Appearance:** Seed and pericarp black; kernel 6.8 mm × 2.5 mm, L/B 2.72. — [Sassikumar et al. 2025, *Scientific Reports* 15:33453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12480474/) [P]. CIKS describes it as "dark brown in colour, bold rice; 1,000-grain weight 42.0 g". — [CIKS profile 15](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Duration conflict:** CIKS gives a 130-day Samba-season crop. A 2025 review table gives 145–150 days. — [CIKS profile 15](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [Anuratha et al. 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Use:** "Ideal for making idly, dosa and dessert… Highly valued by the Chettiar community of Tamil Nadu who offer dishes from this variety on special occasions and for honoured guests." — [CIKS profile 15](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Cooking behaviour:** Water absorption ratio 4.23 g/g, the highest of nine varieties tested. Cooked length 10.0 mm, among the highest length-wise expansion. Intermediate amylose (22%), so "firm and fluffy". The alkali-spread result predicts a longer cooking time. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149) [P]. A separate study found soft gel consistency (111 mm) and a cooked kernel of 9.9 mm (elongation ratio 1.5). — [Sassikumar et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12480474/)
- **Composition (Sassikumar 2025, % of grain):** protein 11.27; crude fibre 2.28; ash 0.89; total starch 64.14; amylose 24.3; resistant starch 8.42; total phenolics 1,055 mg GAE/100 g; zinc 18.8 µg/g; iron 11.5 µg/g. Anthocyanins were not quantified. — [Sci Rep 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12480474/) [P]
- **Composition (CIKS 2019, row "Karuppu Kouni", parboiled, per 100 g):** moisture 12.19 g; ash 2.80 g; protein 8.94 g; fat 1.42 g; dietary fibre 3.64 g; carbohydrate 71.01 g; energy 332.58 kcal; K 254.60 mg; Fe 6.50 mg; Ca 36.90 mg; Mg 112.80 mg; Zn 1.60 mg; P 313.6 mg. — [CIKS Table 9 row 15](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf). The two studies' protein figures differ (11.27% vs 8.94 g), which is typical of different samples and methods, so do not present either as the product's value.
- **Preprint:** A Research Square preprint (not peer-reviewed) reports raw Karuppu Kavuni at 9.42% protein and 6.7% fat. I saw this only through a search snippet. — [Research Square rs-10203006](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10203006/v1) [unverified]
- **Antioxidants:** A 2022 screen of 16 Tamil Nadu varieties concluded that Karuppu Kavuni had higher antioxidant and anthocyanin content than the others. Seen through an abstract or snippet only. — [IJPSS 2022 via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362431401_Evaluation_of_Biochemical_Properties_of_Traditional_Rice_Varieties_of_Tamil_Nadu)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Remedy for dog bites" or "useful against poisons": a traditional belief recorded by CIKS and the AEER review. — [CIKS](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). It is a disease or treatment claim.
- "Prevents cancer, diabetes or heart disease" or "anti-ageing": there is no human evidence in the studies found. The papers measure compounds in the grain, not effects in people.
- "Emperor's/forbidden rice" or "in use since 400 BC": the AEER review asserts the 400 BC date without a primary source, so it is unverified history. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- "Rich in antioxidants": it is a comparative lab finding. FSSAI nutrient-content claims need defined thresholds, and none exists for "antioxidants". Safer wording: "a deep black grain coloured by natural pigments in the bran".

### Inferences
- Because it has the highest measured water absorption, the writer can fairly say it "needs more water and a longer soak than white rice". Exact ratio guidance for Karuppu Kavuni was not found in a credible source.

### Gaps
- No credible soak, water-ratio or cooking-time recommendation specific to Karuppu Kavuni. It is not in the CIKS cooking table. Many sellers advise soaking overnight, but I found no credible source for that.

## 3. Arubatham Kuruvai Rice (Arupatham Kuruvai, அறுபதாம் குறுவை, "sixtieth-day kuruvai")

### Takeaway
A very short-duration red rice. Its name refers to a roughly 60-day crop, but measured durations range from 60–70 to 105 days. Lab data show it is low in amylose (classed as "waxy" in one study), so expect a softer, stickier cook. CIKS's Namma Nellu suggests idli, dosa, idiyappam, puttu, kozhukattai and everyday meals.

### Cited Findings
- **Duration conflict:** Namma Nellu (CIKS) gives 60–70 days, listed as 60, as a "very short duration variety" for all seasons, widely grown in Kanyakumari district. — [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/) [NGO]. The CIKS seed-bank table records 105 days in its own plots. — [CIKS source book, variety table row 7](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf). The AEER review gives 90–100 days. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Appearance:** "Red in colour… medium coarse." — [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/). The AEER review lists it among "slender red rice" types. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). Lab size: 5.48 mm × 2.09 mm, L/B 2.62 (medium), chalky endosperm. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149)
- **Texture:** Amylose 5%, categorised "waxy", so it will be "sticky and waxy on cooking". Cooked length 9.80 mm (high length-wise expansion); water absorption ratio 3.56 g/g. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149) [P]
- **Dishes:** idli, dosa, idiyappam, puttu, meals, kolukattai. Namma Nellu also mentions its "milky nature" for porridge. — [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/)
- **Nutrition:** Namma Nellu cites protein 9.18 g and calcium 35 mg per 100 g, with no lab or method stated. — [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/) [NGO; unverified method]

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Cures anaemia (high iron)", "cures nervous disorders/diseases", "relieves constipation", "suitable for diabetics": stated in the AEER review table and on Namma Nellu, all disease claims. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf); [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/). No measured iron value was found for this variety.
- "For convalescents": this frames the product as therapy. Avoid.

### Inferences
- Since the amylose is very low, a writer could say "soft, slightly sticky when cooked; good for tiffin batters and kozhukattai". That follows from the lab result plus Namma Nellu's dish list.

### Gaps
- No sourced soak time or water ratio. No sourced iron value.

## 4. Poongar Rice (Poonkar, பூங்கார்)

### Takeaway
A traditional Tamil Nadu red rice. In the one lab study found, the grain was slender, with low amylose (softer cook) and among the higher phenolic values of nine varieties. Duration is reported as 120–130 days in one review. Credible dish or cooking guidance is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Colour:** Listed among slender red-rice varieties. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). The Plant Archives study describes its pericarp in the light-brown to dark-red range. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149)
- **Grain:** Kernel 5.68 mm × 1.79 mm, L/B 3.17 (slender), the highest bulk density of the nine varieties, white-belly kernel. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149) [P]
- **Texture:** Amylose 13% (low), so "sticky… on cooking". Cooked length 9.10 mm; water absorption ratio 3.51 g/g. — [Plant Archives 2024](https://doi.org/10.51470/PLANTARCHIVES.2024.v24.no.1.149)
- **Duration:** 120–130 days. — [AEER 2025 table](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). CIKS mentions Poongar as a ~150-day companion crop, but the phrasing is ambiguous. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Market:** CIKS's market survey found Poongar on sale in at least three Chennai stores. — [CIKS source book, market section](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Preprint:** Parboiled Poongar showed the highest protein (9.90%) of the four varieties studied, and raw Poongar had the lowest fat (2.3%). Not peer-reviewed, and read through a snippet only. — [Research Square rs-10203006](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10203006/v1) [unverified]

### Unsafe claims and why
- "For women's vitality", "women's rice", "helps menstrual or fertility problems": the AEER review lists "Poonkaar" as a landrace for "women's vitality / overcome overall health problems". — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). This is a physiological or health claim with no evidence.
- "Rich in antioxidants and good for digestion": from the same review table, and the digestion part is a health claim. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Aromatic" and "nutty" appear only in seller or blog copy. I found no credible source, so leave them out unless the owner's own tasting supports them.

### Gaps
- No credible cooking ratio, soak time, dish list or Tamil-script spelling verified from a primary source. Poongar has no CIKS variety profile in the source book.

## 5. Mappillai Samba (Mani Samba) Rice (மாப்பிள்ளை சம்பா)

### Takeaway
A red, bold, long-duration (about 160 days) Samba-season rice. Its name means "bridegroom". It is cooked as parboiled table rice and used in kanji, aval (poha) appam, idli and dosa. CIKS's sheet: soak 3 h, rice to water 1:3, 4 whistles.

### Cited Findings
- **Identity:** Samba season; 160-day crop; average height 137 cm. "Red in colour, bold rice"; 1,000-grain weight 31.0 g. "The word 'mapillai' means 'bridegroom'. It is said that the Kanji was… sought after by young men seeking brides!" — [CIKS profile 24](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO]. The AEER table gives 150–160 days. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Dishes:** "Table rice for boiled rice lovers. Can be mixed with aval (Poha) and aval aappam can be made. Also good for dosa and idly." — [CIKS profile 24](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf). It is listed among landraces used for puffed or popped rice. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Cooking:** Rice to water 1:3; 4 whistles; coarse texture; soak 3 hours before cooking. — [CIKS cooking-instructions table, row 5](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO]
- **Composition (CIKS 2019, parboiled, per 100 g):** moisture 11.92 g; ash 1.75 g; protein 7.91 g; fat 2.18 g; dietary fibre 1.79 g; carbohydrate 74.45 g; energy 349 kcal; K 299.90 mg; Fe 6.90 mg; Ca 43.60 mg; Mg 117.90 mg; Zn 1.90 mg; P 334.0 mg. — [CIKS Table 9 row 26](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Phenolics:** One cited study found total phenol 39 mg/g, the highest among the six varieties listed, including Poongar at 13. — [AEER 2025, table citing an earlier study](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- **Metabolomics:** A 2022 study in *Plants* 11(4):543 compared the grain metabolome with a white-rice line and reported compounds such as phytosterols, flavonoids and polyamines. This identifies compounds present in the grain, not effects in people. Seen through an abstract or snippet. — [Rajagopalan, Manickam & Muthurajan 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8876031/)
- **"Mani Samba":** I found no source linking "Mani Samba" as a synonym of Mappillai Samba. Treat it as the store's own alias and verify it with the supplier.

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Increases stamina, strength or virility", "increases sperm count", "men's vitality": CIKS and the AEER review record the bridegroom folklore and say it "increases sperm production". — [CIKS profile 24](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). These are physiological claims. The name story can be told as folklore ("the name means bridegroom"), without any implied effect.
- "Heals mouth or stomach ulcers", "strengthens nerves", "good for diabetes": stated in the AEER table, all disease claims. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf)
- "Low GI": the CIKS study gave 68.84 against White Ponni = 100, a relative value. On the glucose scale that is not "low", and in any case the result comes from one study. — [CIKS Table 8](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)

### Inferences
- Its long 160-day duration and red bold grain are good honest differentiators.

### Gaps
- No sourced shelf life.

## 6. Iluppai Poo Samba Rice (இலுப்பைப் பூ சம்பா)

### Takeaway
A white, fine-grained Samba-season rice. It is named for a fragrance likened to the iluppai (mahua, *Madhuca*) flower. CIKS describes it as "ideal for making kheer" (payasam). It is sold as raw rice.

### Cited Findings
- **Identity:** Samba season; 120-day crop, average height 110 cm; "White in colour, fine rice"; 1,000-grain weight 19.0 g. "The grain has a fragrance like Madhuca flower (Iluppai Poo in Tamil)." — [CIKS profile 4](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf) [NGO]
- **Duration conflict:** The AEER review gives 125–135 days. — [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). The same review also lists Ilupaipoo Samba among "black rice varieties". That contradicts CIKS's "white" and looks like an error in the review.
- **Dishes:** "Ideal for making Kheer." — [CIKS profile 4](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf)
- **Composition (CIKS 2019, raw rice, per 100 g):** moisture 11.14 g; ash 0.54 g; protein 9.47 g; fat 1.85 g; dietary fibre below the detection limit (<1.0 g); carbohydrate 77.00 g; energy 362.53 kcal; K 86.60 mg; Fe 3.40 mg; Ca 22.10 mg; Mg 28.60 mg; Zn 1.80 mg; P 119.1 mg. — [CIKS Table 9 row 4](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf). The profile text says "high in iron, calcium and zinc", but the table values are lower than the parboiled red varieties'. Avoid the "high in iron" wording.

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Useful for diabetes and knee pain", "kanji helps paralysis (pakshavatham)", "relieves joint pain", "immune-boosting", "stabilises bones", "for anaemic patients": these are recorded as beliefs ("said to be") by CIKS, and as facts in the AEER review. — [CIKS profile 4](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf); [AEER 2025](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). All are disease or health claims.

### Inferences
- The naming story and the kheer use are the strongest honest copy. Fine white raw rice usually needs less water than the parboiled red varieties, but I found no sourced ratio for this variety.

### Gaps
- No sourced cooking ratio or soak time.

## 7. Vellai Kavuni (White Kavuni Rice, வெள்ளைக் கவுனி)

### Takeaway

## 8. Paneer Rose (dried rose petals, *Rosa damascena* / "panneer roja")

### Takeaway
Damask rose (*Rosa damascena*) petals are used in India for gulkand (a rose-petal and sugar preserve), rose syrup, rose water and rose tea. Dried petals are made by shade-drying fresh, pesticide-free petals. Food use requires culinary-grade, unsprayed petals.

### Cited Findings
- **Species and uses:** "Rose petals of *R. bourboniana* and *R. damascena* are used for making Gulkhand." Value-added rose products include rose petal jam, rose syrup, rose floral tea, rose RTS (ready-to-serve drink), rose wine and rose water. — [NIFTEM / PMFME "Processing of Rose" deck](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf) [P, government food-processing training material]
- **Gulkand:** Petals and sugar (1 kg petals to 1.25–2 kg sugar) are sun-cured for about 3–4 weeks. It is used in paan, sweets and rose drinks. — [NIFTEM](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf); [Tarla Dalal glossary](https://www.tarladalal.com/glossary-gulkand-flavoured-rose-petals-464i) [S]
- **Drying:** Petals of flowers "not treated with pesticides" were spread thin and shade-dried at room temperature (25–27 °C). The dried material was stored in hermetically closed containers. — [NIFTEM rose tea flow chart](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)
- **Culinary grade:** Recipe sources advise using only unsprayed, edible-grade petals. — [mygingergarlickitchen rose syrup](https://mygingergarlickitchen.com/PRINT-RECIPES/RECIPE-PRINT-rose-syrup.html) [W]
- **Indian growing states:** Rose is grown in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and others. — [NIFTEM](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Kidney tonic", "regulates menstruation", "good for heart, liver and uterus", "treats acne", "reduces body heat, eye inflammation", "strengthens gums", "rose tea controls bleeding or bladder infection": the NIFTEM deck itself lists these. That shows even government training material repeats folk claims, but they remain disease or health claims with no cited evidence. — [NIFTEM](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)

### Inferences
- Honest copy: aroma, colour, uses (gulkand, rose tea, rose milk, garnish for sweets and biryani), and "store airtight away from light". The last is common practice but unsourced.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Tamil common name "panneer roja" against a botanical reference, nor confirm that the store's petals are *R. damascena* (ask the supplier). No sourced shelf life for dried petals, and no measured nutrition for dried petals.

## 9. Hibiscus Flower (dried): which species?

### Takeaway
Two different plants are sold as "hibiscus". *Hibiscus rosa-sinensis* (Tamil *semparutti*, the red shoe-flower) is the garden hibiscus used in South Indian home remedies and hair oils. *Hibiscus sabdariffa* (roselle) is the species whose fleshy red calyces make the tart "hibiscus tea" sold worldwide. The supplier must confirm which one the store sells. The safety data for rosa-sinensis is thin, and animal studies flag effects on fertility.

### Cited Findings
- ***H. rosa-sinensis*:** Tamil name Semparutti, Hindi Gurhal, English "shoe flower" or Chinese hibiscus. Five-petalled flower about 10 cm across, red from anthocyanins (cyanidin-3-O-glucoside), which are used as food colourants. Used in jams, soups and teas. — [Raza et al. 2025, *Food Science & Nutrition* 13(12):e71254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/) [P]
- **Flower composition (fresh, ranges):** moisture 76–83%, protein 1.54–2.4%, carbohydrate 13–15%, fibre 1.5–2%. — [Raza et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/). These are fresh-flower ranges, not dried-product values.
- **Safety, *H. rosa-sinensis*:** Evidence is mostly animal or in vitro, and the review states human clinical data are "essentially absent". Traditional uses include emmenagogue and abortifacient. Flower extract at 300 mg/kg for 30–60 days caused antifertility effects in male rats. — [Raza et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/)
- ***H. sabdariffa*:** The most commonly consumed part is the fleshy, deep-red calyx. It is used mainly for infusions ("hibiscus tea"), jams and syrups. — [Biology Insights](https://biologyinsights.com/how-to-eat-hibiscus-flowers-leaves-and-tea/) [W/S]. Rosa-sinensis flowers are milder and are used more as garnish than as a staple. — [Biology Insights, "Which hibiscus are not edible"](https://biologyinsights.com/which-hibiscus-are-not-edible-and-why/) [W]
- A US clinical-trial protocol exists for an *H. sabdariffa* beverage. That shows it is studied as a drink, but it is not evidence of benefit for a product page. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05774613 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/13/NCT05774613/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Lowers blood pressure", "controls diabetes or cholesterol", "cures hair fall", "regulates periods", "anti-cancer": all are disease or physiological claims. For rosa-sinensis the evidence is preclinical only. — [Raza et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/)
- Do not imply the product is safe for pregnancy or fertility. Animal antifertility findings exist and there are no human data. — [Raza et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658387/)

### Inferences
- If the product is dried rosa-sinensis petals (the usual "sembaruthi poo" in Tamil shops), describe it as a petal for herbal infusions or colour, with a neutral note such as "consult a doctor if pregnant". If it is roselle calyx, describe the tart, cranberry-like infusion. The flavour description is common knowledge and was not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No FSSAI-specific standard or approval found for either species as a dried flower. No measured nutrition for dried petals or calyces from IFCT. The tea-preparation ratio is unsourced.

## 10. Aavaram Flower (Avarampoo, *Senna auriculata*, syn. *Cassia auriculata*)

### Takeaway
Tanner's cassia: a shrub with bright-yellow flowers about 4–5 cm, borne in clusters of 2–8. The dried flowers and powder are traditionally taken as a tea or drink in South India. Pharmacological research is almost entirely in animals. The plant also shows drug-interaction signals in rats.

### Cited Findings
- **Names:** English Tanner's senna or cassia, "Mature tea tree"; Sanskrit Avartaki; Hindi Tarwar or Awal; Telugu Tangedu. The review gives no Tamil name. — [Nille et al. 2021, *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 12:647887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/) [P]. The Tamil "avaram/avarampoo" is in common use, but this review does not confirm it.
- **Flower:** Bright yellow, irregular, large (4–5 cm), in axillary racemes of 2–8. — [Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/)
- **Traditional use:** Dried flower powder and flower tea are consumed; the dried flowers and buds are used as a substitute for regular tea. — [Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/); [Wikipedia: Senna auriculata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senna_auriculata) [S]
- **Safety:** In rats, flower extracts caused no mortality up to 3,000 mg/kg. There are no human safety data. Flower tea raised theophylline levels by 32.5% and carbamazepine by 47.1% in rats, and an extract reduced metformin absorption by 60%. The authors call for clinical and drug-interaction studies. — [Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/)
- **Regulatory view of extracts:** Traditional preparations have been considered safe, but solvent-based extracts "should be recognized as novel entities". — [Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Controls or cures diabetes", "anti-diabetic flower", "reduces sugar levels": the most common marketing claim. It is a disease claim supported only by preclinical work and two small clinical reports. — [Nille et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423098/)
- "Glowing skin", "cures urinary infection or body heat": these are folk claims, with no human evidence found.
- The drug-interaction signals with metformin, theophylline and carbamazepine are rat data. Even so, a page aimed at people with diabetes would steer the very people on metformin toward it. Avoid any "for diabetics" positioning, and consider adding "consult your doctor if on medication".

### Inferences
- Honest copy: "Sun-yellow avaram flowers, dried for a traditional South Indian herbal infusion (avarampoo tea)."

### Gaps
- No FSSAI status found. No measured nutrition. No sourced brewing ratio or shelf life.

## 11. Butterfly Pea Flower (Sangu poo, *Clitoria ternatea*, dried)

### Takeaway
The deep-blue flower is used mainly as a natural blue colour and as an infusion that turns purple-pink when acid such as lemon is added. Its regulatory status differs by market. The US FDA permits butterfly pea flower extract as a colour additive. EFSA (EU) raised safety objections in 2022 to the dried flowers as a "traditional food", citing a lack of data on cyclotides.

### Cited Findings
- **US:** 21 CFR 73.69 approved butterfly pea flower extract as a colour additive in 2021, for beverages, teas, sports drinks and similar products. It was expanded in May 2025 to cereals, crackers, snack mixes and others. The principal colouring components are anthocyanins. — [21 CFR 73.69 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/73.69); [Federal Register 12 May 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-12/html/2025-08248.htm) [P]
- **EU:** On 2 Feb 2022, EFSA raised safety objections to dried *C. ternatea* flowers as a traditional food. It cited in vitro haemolytic and cytotoxic effects of some cyclotides and limited data. It also said the claimed traditional infusion use could not be verified. — [EFSA Supporting Publication EN-7084 (2022)](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/sp.efsa.2022.EN-7084); [3E summary](https://www.3eco.com/article/efsa-raises-concern-about-use-of-clitoria-ternatea-l-flowers-as-traditional-food/) [P/S]
- **Uses:** Used in Southeast Asia in herbal infusions and as a food colorant "without apparent adverse health effects", in the applicant's account summarised by 3E. — [3E](https://www.3eco.com/article/efsa-raises-concern-about-use-of-clitoria-ternatea-l-flowers-as-traditional-food/)
- **Evidence base:** A 2026 review describes human evidence as restricted to small acute crossover studies and exploratory adjunctive studies. Seen through a search snippet. — [PMC review, C. ternatea phytopharmacology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13719572)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Boosts memory or brain power (medhya)", "reduces anxiety", "anti-diabetic", "anti-cancer", "improves eyesight": these are common claims, but human evidence is minimal. — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13719572)
- "Safe and approved worldwide" is false. The EU objected and there is no EU authorisation. — [EFSA 2022](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/sp.efsa.2022.EN-7084)

### Inferences
- Honest copy: "A blue flower for brewing a colour-changing tea: add lemon and it turns violet." The colour change is anthocyanin chemistry and is consistent with the FDA listing of anthocyanins as the colouring principle. Keep the language to colour, flavour and use.

### Gaps
- FSSAI status not found. The Tamil name "sangu poo" (சங்குப் பூ) is common usage but was not verified from a botanical source here. No measured nutrition. No sourced brewing ratio or shelf life.

## 12. Organic Real Rose Milk (rose syrup or drink)

### Takeaway
Rose syrup is made from rose-petal extract and sugar. A government (NIFTEM/PMFME) process uses about 1 kg petals to 1.5 kg sugar, with citric acid, and optionally sodium benzoate as a preservative. It is mixed into chilled milk to make "rose milk". Under FSSAI the product sits in the synthetic syrup or sharbat standard, and "organic" requires certification.

### Cited Findings
- **Process:** 1 kg petals boiled for 15 minutes in 1 L water, drained, then 1.5 kg sugar and 15 g citric acid added. A low-cost method adds 3–5 g sodium benzoate per batch. The bottles are pasteurised at 82 °C. "Rose syrup can be stored for a long time and can be used instead of synthetic flavour for making rose milk, cakes and marshmallows." — [NIFTEM/PMFME rose processing](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf) [P]
- **Market context:** NIFTEM notes most commercial rose products use synthetic colour. — [NIFTEM](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)
- **FSSAI standard (needs verification):** The older 2.3.24 "synthetic syrup / sharbat" text required total soluble solids of at least 65%. A 2018 draft proposed that sharbat contain at least 25% fruit juice or extract or the named material, with TSS above 30% and pH below 4.5. The final status of the 2019 amendment was not confirmed. — [FSSAI draft notice 17 Jul 2018](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Draft_Notice_Comments_Synthetic_Syrup_17_07_2018.pdf); [FSSAI press release 19 Mar 2019](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Draft%20Notification/Press_Release_Draft_Notification_Synthetic_Syrup_19_03_2019.pdf); [Food Safety Helpline](https://foodsafetyhelpline.com/fssai-compliance/synthetic-syrup-or-sharbat/) [P/S]
- **"Organic":** Requires NPOP or PGS certification and the Jaivik Bharat logo. — [FSSAI organic page](https://fssai.gov.in/cms/organic-food.php)

### Unsafe claims and why
- "Cooling for the body", "reduces body heat", "good for digestion or acidity": traditional or Ayurvedic framing of a health effect with no evidence. — [NIFTEM lists similar gulkand claims](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/roseprocessing.pdf)
- "No preservatives" or "no colour": only if true of the actual formulation. The NIFTEM process shows sodium benzoate is common.
- "Real rose": acceptable only if the product is made from rose petals or extract and not flavour alone. The ingredient list must support it.

### Inferences
- The page should state the serving method (for example, X ml syrup to Y ml chilled milk, per the label) and "refrigerate after opening". Both should come from the product label, not from this research.

### Gaps
- The product's actual ingredients, colour source, TSS and shelf life are unknown and must come from the label or manufacturer.

## 14. Hand-pounded Rice Idiyappam Flour

### Takeaway
Idiyappam (string hoppers) is made by pressing a dough of rice flour and hot water through a mould, then steaming it. Traditional Tamil sources name specific rice varieties as suited to idiyappam. The product page should say which rice is used, whether the flour is raw or roasted, and that "hand-pounded" means less polished rice. Apart from rice-variety suitability, I found no credible sourced data for this product.

### Cited Findings
- **Varieties for idiyappam:** A 2025 review compiles landraces "used for" idiyappam: Raamakurikar and others, plus Kaivara Samba, Madumuzhungi, Tulasi vaasanai samba and Vaasanai Jeeraga Samba. Some names were cut off in the extraction. — [AEER 2025, product-use table](https://www.aloki.hu/pdf/2301_325342.pdf). CIKS lists Kaatuyanam as "ideal for making idly, dosa, idiyappam and kheer". — [CIKS profile 16](https://ciks.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Traditional-Rice-Varieties-of-Tamilnadu_compressed.pdf). Namma Nellu lists idiyappam among Arubatham Kuruvai's uses. — [Namma Nellu](https://nammanellu.com/rice-variety/arubatham-kuruvai/)
- **Hand-pounding:** The CIKS 2019 analysis compared a hand-pounded Kuzhiyadichan sample (10.21 g protein, 2.98 g fat, 1.84 g fibre per 100 g) with a parboiled-milled one (9.73 g protein, 2.57 g fat, 2.35 g fibre). - "Easily digestible, ideal for the sick or babies", "diabetic-friendly", "gluten-free so good for celiacs". Rice is naturally gluten-free, but a gluten-free claim on Indian labels has its own FSSAI requirements and cross-contact testing; I did not research them, so flag this for checking before use.

### Inferences
- If the flour is made from one of the store's heritage rices, say which. That is the most distinctive honest copy.

### Gaps
- No sourced flour-to-water ratio, preparation method, or shelf life for rice flour (it goes rancid faster than whole rice because of bran oils, but I found no source for this here). IFCT values for rice flour were not retrieved.
